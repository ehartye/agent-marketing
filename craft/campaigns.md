# Campaign and initiative craft

The campaign is a testable path from a specific person's attention to a useful next action. Zukowski's [game campaign guide](https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/07/12/how-to-market-your-indie-game-a-10-step-plan/) connects audience, comparable games, differentiation and a clear funnel; its Steam thresholds and dated mechanics do not transfer as universal requirements.

Write one initiative record per channel/test: audience, hypothesis, native asset, CTA, owner, start/end, budget and status. Put detailed assumptions in a companion brief. Plan backwards from the review decision: what observation would cause continuing, changing the message or stopping? Choose a primary outcome near actual product value; keep supporting platform attention metrics separate.

For a three-hours-per-week maker, a sensible **house allocation** is one hour preparing a reusable demonstration, one hour contributing/responding where the audience already participates, and one hour reviewing results. Change this allocation when evidence shows the bottleneck is the product, support or distribution. Time estimates are effort caps, not promises.

Before launch, verify the try path on the audience's device, check channel rules, use [consistent post-level UTM tags](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952?hl=en), prepare replies to likely objections, and confirm support capacity. Drafts may be generated under a marketing request. Posting, spending and contacting people require the owner's instruction for those actions; an initiative record is not authorization to execute them.

At review, state spend and maker hours, attributed first use, returning cohort if available, observed objections, uncertainty and one next action. Do not change the success threshold after seeing results. Keep failed and paused initiatives visible so the family does not repeat a disproven approach.
