# Channels and approaches

[Channel cards](../library/channels.json) include audience cues, category, effort, cost, risk, complexity and maintenance. `marketing channels <project-id>` orders these with a transparent house heuristic: +3 category match, +2 audience cue, +1 within weekly time or −2 over it, +1 when the card suits the project's stage or −2 when it does not, and, for a game that has not launched, −1 on a store that charges before release. A project with no usable stage is scored on the first three terms alone. This is a starting hypothesis, not a forecast. Audience membership, format quality and current local norms matter more than the score.

| Project and situation | First hypothesis to test | Evidence that changes the choice |
|---|---|---|
| Developer tool with a working example | GitHub docs/example, then an eligible Show HN | Successful installs, useful objections, repeat use |
| Playable game with readable genre/payoff | Player community or video of the actual loop | Qualified plays, first-round completion, return cohort |
| Free browser game, no store page yet | A portal's own test (CrazyGames Basic Launch, Poki's fit tests) or an itch.io page, plus one hand-picked press or creator batch | Plays, playtime and a day-1 return rate for one cohort |
| Educational app | Intended learners/parents and useful teaching examples | Observed learning task, accessibility, repeated use |
| Professional workflow app | Specific professional community and before/after demo | Completed workflow and credible interest at a price |
| Existing users | Opt-in email or the community they already use | Reactivation, useful replies, unsubscribes |

These recommendations are original planning inferences. No bundled source proves a universal best channel. SEO introduces a longer feedback lag; a launch community can respond quickly but needs active support. A new Discord server adds ongoing moderation before demand is established. Paid promotion adds spending risk and requires a useful outcome measure before scaling.

Check [Show HN eligibility](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html): substantive maker work people can try, rather than a signup-only landing page or a quickly generated one-off. Inspect each community's own rules as well as [Reddit's spam policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam). Do not turn the CLI into a promotion bot. [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/launch) permits maker launches but prohibits upvote solicitation. Platform rules and access can change; check them on the actual planning date.

## By stage

A project's `stage` changes what a first test can be. Each card lists the stages where its own approach can be carried out (`stages` in the library), and `marketing advise` names the work that fits the stage. The order is a house planning inference, not a published sequence; the rules behind it are `marketing rules stage` and, for games, `marketing rules games`.

| Stage | What a test can be | Cards that fit | `advise` leads with |
|---|---|---|---|
| idea | Nothing can be tried, so only desire can be observed: conversations about the problem and what people use now | Reddit and Discord, where a question is welcome | Check the promise matters before building more; listen where the audience already talks |
| prototype | Intended strangers reaching and finishing a first session | Adds itch.io, Newgrounds, Game Jolt, video and social channels, GitHub, Show HN, search, LinkedIn | Get intended strangers to a first session, until starts are recorded |
| beta | Widening on the channel with the best first-session ratio, while the first session is fixed | Adds the stores, Poki, CrazyGames, press, email and Product Hunt | Widen through the best-evidenced channel |
| launched | Who returns, what players say, and an update cadence the maker can keep | Every card | Read who returns and what players say |

For a game, a venue that is free to enter comes before a paid store page: Steam charges $100 per product that is recouped only after $1,000 adjusted gross revenue, Apple $99 a year and Google Play $25 once, while an itch.io page and the portals' own tests cost nothing to enter. The three cards that charge before release carry `paidEntry` and are scored −1 until the game has launched. A store page then opens only after its readiness gates pass; `marketing plan` reads the gates and `advise` names the ones that are open or failed.

## TikTok

TikTok suits a game, creative project or app whose payoff shows in a few seconds of real play or use: a short vertical clip with one tryable next step in the profile link or caption. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test, not a finding. Parent and learner audiences are reachable, but do not target or collect data from children; read the platform's age and minor-safety rules before building a campaign around young viewers.

- **Rules.** A post that promotes a brand, product or service needs the [content disclosure setting](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-content-disclosure-setting-for-creators) on; the page notes that legal requirements vary by country and that the US FTC Endorsement Guides apply. Confirm in the app how promoting your own app is labelled. Bots, bulk automation and bought or traded engagement are prohibited and manipulative engagement bait can lose For You eligibility ([integrity policy](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity), whose live text you should read before relying on exact wording). Do not run a posting bot.
- **Evidence.** Record the platform's own measure in `definition`. TikTok's [ads-side definitions](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/video-play?lang=en) count video views as playback starts with replays excluded, and "video views at 100%" as full-length plays; in-app creator analytics use different terms and may not match, so copy the label you actually read. Compare watch measures only between videos of similar length. Views and full plays are attention. A tagged profile-link visit is the first step toward use; first use, return and payment still come from your own product analytics (see [the example import](../examples/observations-tiktok.csv)).
- **First test.** One clip format, three to five videos over two weeks, one tagged link, a stated stopping rule. A cluster of views with no tagged visits is a finding about the clip or the link, not a reason to buy engagement.

## Facebook

Facebook suits a family-facing or community-led project: a game, creative project or learning app whose people gather in Groups or follow a Page, including parents and local communities. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. Organic reach is not guaranteed and varies by Group and Page, so measure it rather than assume it.

- **Rules.** Meta's [spam standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/) prohibits buying, selling or exchanging engagement, posting at very high frequencies manually or automatically, and misleading links. Read each Group's own rules before posting; a Group admin can remove promotion that Meta would allow. If you promote someone else's product for value, Meta requires the Paid Partnership label ([branded content](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/213764212711862), read from search extracts; confirm the live page). Promoting your own product is not branded content, but say plainly that it is yours. Do not run a posting bot.
- **Evidence.** Meta defines [Page impressions as times content entered a person's screen and reach as an estimated count of people](https://www.facebook.com/help/274400362581037), and has announced Views as the replacement for impressions on non-video content, so copy the label your export actually shows into `definition`. The ledger has no people-reached metric: record exposure as `impressions` or `views`, and keep reach in the definition note. Exposure is not first use. A tagged link visit is the first step; use, return and payment come from your own analytics (see [the example import](../examples/observations-facebook.csv)).
- **First test.** One relevant Group where you already participate, one honest post with a tagged link, a replies-and-objections log and a stated stopping rule. Silence is a result about fit or wording, not a reason to boost the post.

## X

X suits a tool, app, game or creative project whose audience already talks in builder or genre communities, where a short demonstration and a tryable link can start a conversation. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. It is a weak fit for audiences that are not on X, such as school-age students, and does not suit a long-lag launch.

- **Rules.** The [developer policy](https://docs.x.com/developer-terms/policy) prohibits using the API for spam or platform manipulation, bulk or aggressive actions, and automated replies or direct messages without explicit consent, and requires API bots to say what they are and who runs them. The [authenticity rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity) (read from search extracts; confirm the live page) also prohibit bulk or unsolicited replies and mentions and engagement trading. Post and reply by hand. Do not automate replies, likes or follows, and do not scrape. Access, terms and pricing for the API have changed repeatedly, so check them again before any API use.
- **Evidence.** The API [counts an impression each time a post appears on a screen and does not de-duplicate](https://docs.x.com/x-api/fundamentals/metrics), and the Ads export defines impressions and engagements differently, so copy the label you exported into `definition`. Non-public metrics are only available for posts from the last 30 days: export on a schedule, because older data is lost. Impressions are exposure, not first use. A tagged link visit is the first step; use, return and payment come from your own analytics (see [the example import](../examples/observations-x.csv)).
- **First test.** Three to five posts of one demonstration format over two weeks, one tagged link, replies handled by hand and logged, and a stated stopping rule. Impressions with no tagged visits is a finding about the post or the link, not a reason to buy engagement.

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn suits a professional-workflow tool or app whose users are identifiable by job, with a credible before/after demonstration. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. It is a poor fit for games, school-age learners and family projects.

- **Rules.** The [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies) prohibit using invitations to send promotional messages to people you do not know, untargeted or repetitive promotional messages, and artificially increasing engagement. LinkedIn's [prohibited software page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387/prohibited-software-and-extensions?lang=en) bans crawlers, bots and browser extensions that scrape or automate activity, and says accounts can be restricted or shut down. Do not use outreach automation or a posting bot, and do not message people you do not know to promote the product.
- **Evidence.** [LinkedIn's Page analytics](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a564051) count an impression when a post is on screen for 300 milliseconds with half of it in view, as an estimate, and define clicks without reactions, comments or reposts. The same help site defines engagements differently across pages, so copy the label and the page you used into `definition`. The ledger has no people-reached metric: record exposure as `impressions` and keep "members reached" in the definition note. Impressions are exposure, not first use. A tagged link visit and a qualified conversation are the first steps; use and payment come from your own analytics (see [the example import](../examples/observations-linkedin.csv)).
- **First test.** Two or three posts showing one real workflow with permission-based user stories over two weeks, one tagged link, replies handled by hand, and a stated stopping rule. Many impressions with no qualified conversations is a finding about relevance or the demo, not a reason to buy ads.

## Steam

Steam is a destination store, not a place to advertise: it contains no paid advertising, and developers who want traffic must send it there from elsewhere. That makes it different from the other channel cards. Record store-page results under channel `steam`, and keep the source of each visitor in `utm_source` so the other channels can be compared by what they actually deliver. It suits games only; a study app or a tool has no use for it.

- **Plan the clock first.** [Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect) charges $100 per product (recoupable after $1,000 adjusted gross revenue), requires bank, tax and identity verification and a 1-5 day review, waits 30 days after payment before a first release, and needs a public coming-soon page for at least two weeks before launch. Open the page early, because wishlist evidence only grows while the page is up. Confirm current fees and waits in Steamworks before paying.
- **Rules.** [Steam has no paid ads inside it](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/advertising); paid campaigns run off Steam and point to the store page. Read Valve's store-page and community rules before writing page text or posting in Steam Discussions. Do not buy wishlists or run bots.
- **Evidence.** [Wishlist reporting](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist/reporting) gives additions, deletions and purchases from the wishlist, updated daily for the previous date. Record additions and deletions as separate `wishlists` readings with their definition, and wishlist purchases as `purchases`. A wishlist is intent, not payment. [UTM analytics](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/utm_analytics) counts only visits by users logged in to Steam, and conversions only within 72 hours of the click, so treat tracked visits as a lower bound and compare sources with each other, not with total traffic (see [the example import](../examples/observations-steam.csv)).
- **First test.** Tag the link from one other channel with `utm_source`, send it to the coming-soon page, and compare wishlist additions and tracked visits per source after a fixed period, with a stated stopping rule. Many tracked visits with few wishlists is a finding about the page, not the channel.

## itch.io and game communities

itch.io suits a small game with a playable build that you can let strangers try before they commit. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test; it is a poor fit for a tool or a study app.

- **Rules.** The [Creator FAQ](https://itch.io/docs/creators/faq) says not to sign people up to mailing lists automatically, and that the buyer email tool is for news specifically related to the transaction. I found no itch.io page on promotion limits, so read the current terms and each community's own rules before posting. Do not run a posting or outreach bot, and do not message strangers in bulk.
- **Evidence.** The [dashboard analytics](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-started) cover views, downloads and purchases. [Referrers](https://itch.io/updates/referrers-and-share-buttons) show where visitors come from, and the [purchases list](https://itch.io/updates/view-where-purchases-come-from) shows Buy location and Referrer. Those posts are from 2013 and 2014, so confirm the screens you actually see. itch.io reports file delivery and referrals, not play sessions: record a download under `starts` with that definition and keep completion and returning players in the game's own telemetry. Views are exposure, not play (see [the example import](../examples/observations-itch.csv)).
- **First test.** Publish one demo page, post it to one relevant community with a tagged link, and compare views, downloads and referrers per source after two weeks, with a stated stopping rule. Many views with few downloads is a finding about the page, not the community.

## Existing Discord community

Discord suits a game, tool or creative project whose likely users already gather in a community around a related interest. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. Start in a community that exists and welcomes the topic; an empty server of your own only adds moderation work.

- **Rules.** Discord's [Community Guidelines](https://discord.com/guidelines) say not to send unsolicited bulk messages, not to use self-bots or user-bots, and not to inflate server membership or manipulate engagement metrics. The [Platform Manipulation explainer](https://discord.com/safety/platform-manipulation-policy-explainer) adds that join-for-join and invite-reward schemes and bought engagement are prohibited. Read the target server's own rules on self-promotion, ask a moderator first, and post as yourself. Do not run a posting or outreach bot, and do not send direct messages to members you do not know.
- **Evidence.** As a guest you see no Discord analytics, so count what your own links and notes show: tagged clicks, playtest sign-ups and replies. Log replies by hand as `comments` with the themes in the definition. If you own a qualifying server, [Server Insights](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032807371-Server-Insights-FAQ) has Growth and Activation, Engagement and Audience groups, but I could only confirm that page from search extracts. A member count is not use (see [the example import](../examples/observations-discord.csv)).
- **First test.** Ask permission, post one concrete playtest or feedback request with a tagged link in one relevant server, answer every reply by hand, and compare sign-ups and returning testers after a week, with a stated stopping rule. Silence is a finding about fit or the request, not a reason to post more widely.

## Opt-in email

Email suits people who have already asked to hear from you, and is a follow-up channel, not a discovery one. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test.

- **Rules.** Collect consent by a positive action. The UK [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/) rejects pre-ticked boxes and silence, and requires a simple, free opt-out in every message. The US [CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) requires accurate headers, a truthful subject line, a postal address, an opt-out explanation and honouring opt-outs within 10 business days. [Gmail](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en) and [Yahoo](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/) ask bulk senders for SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe and spam rates below 0.3% (Gmail recommends 0.10%). Do not buy lists or run an outreach bot; write to people who opted in.
- **Evidence.** Record tagged clicks and successful use. Opens are weak: [Apple's Mail Privacy Protection](https://support.apple.com/guide/mail/use-mail-privacy-protection-mlhl03be2866/16.0/mac/14.0) downloads remote content when mail arrives, so an open count can include messages nobody read (my inference). Record unsubscribes as `unsubscribes` from your sending tool (see [the example import](../examples/observations-email.csv)).
- **First test.** Send one useful update to a small opted-in list with a tagged link and a working unsubscribe, then compare successful use and unsubscribes after a week against a stated stopping rule. Many opens with no use is not a result.

## GitHub

GitHub suits a developer tool whose first use is reading, cloning or installing code, with a README and quickstart that actually work. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. It is a poor fit for a game aimed at players or an app whose users never open a repository.

- **Rules.** GitHub's [Acceptable Use Policies](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies) ban automated excessive bulk activity, coordinated inauthentic activity and rank abuse such as automated starring, and bar rewards for fake engagement. Promotional text in a README must relate to the hosted project, and promotion in other people's issues is not allowed. Do not buy stars, run starring bots or post links into strangers' issues.
- **Evidence.** [A star](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/exploring-projects-on-github/saving-repositories-with-stars) saves a repository and shows appreciation, and it can be removed, so it is a weak interest signal. [Traffic](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/viewing-activity-and-data-for-your-repository/viewing-traffic-to-a-repository) shows views, unique visitors, referring sites, popular content and full clones for only the past 14 days, and [the REST API](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/metrics/traffic?apiVersion=2026-03-10) needs write access. This plugin's GitHub adapter records `stars` from the public repository read, and with `GITHUB_TOKEN` and traffic enabled also daily `views`, `uniqueVisitors` and `clones`. Referrers and popular paths are not recorded, so copy them by hand before they expire. Daily uniques cannot be summed into people across days. Installs and quickstart successes come from your own analytics (see [the example import](../examples/observations-github.csv)).
- **First test.** Put the quickstart first in the README, run it on a clean machine, tag one link from another channel to the repository, and collect traffic weekly for four weeks. Stop if clones and successful quickstarts stay flat while views rise: that is a finding about the README, not a reason to chase stars.

## Show HN

Show HN suits a developer or maker tool, or an app, that people can run or try immediately and that you built and will discuss. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. It is a poor fit for a signup-only page, a game aimed at players, or a project that is not yet usable.

- **Rules.** The [Show HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) ask for non-trivial work people can run or hold, from someone around to discuss it, easy to try without signups or emails, and say not to ask friends to upvote or comment. Blog posts, landing pages and routine version bumps do not qualify. The [site guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html) say not to use HN primarily for promotion and not to solicit upvotes or comments. The [FAQ](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsfaq.html) says flags and anti-abuse software affect ranking, and reposts are tolerated only rarely. Do not coordinate votes or delete and resubmit.
- **Evidence.** The [official API](https://github.com/HackerNews/API) gives an item's `score` and `descendants` (total comment count). The adapter records those as `votes` and `comments` snapshots for the item id you register, and reads up to 100 comments as text for you to review by hand. Points are not readers, and the FAQ says some votes are dropped. Visits and activation come from your own analytics through a tagged link (see [the example import](../examples/observations-hacker-news.csv)).
- **First test.** One Show HN linking straight to the tryable project, posted when you can answer for several hours, with one tagged link. Read comments for objections and failed first runs, and compare activations after 48 hours against a stated threshold. Few points with good objections is a result; do not repost to fix it.

## Product Hunt

Product Hunt suits a new, usable tool or app aimed at makers and early adopters, with a maker who can answer comments for the whole launch day. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. It is a poor fit for a closed beta, a game for players, or a product the community already knows.

- **Rules.** [How Product Hunt works](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/how-product-hunt-works) says a launch must be new, a product and usable, and that company or brand accounts cannot post or comment. Do not pay anyone to hunt or send traffic. [Sharing your launch](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/sharing-your-launch) says to ask for feedback, not upvotes, and that contests rewarding upvotes can get the product unfeatured. Do not mass-message or send scraped emails.
- **Preparation.** [The preparation guide](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/preparing-for-launch) wants a tagline under 60 characters, a description up to 500, at least two gallery images and a first comment asking for feedback. Its submission link cannot be shortened or a tracking link.
- **Evidence.** The leaderboard moves on upvotes, comments, time and undisclosed factors, so rank is not demand. The plugin has no Product Hunt adapter: record `votes` and `comments` by hand from the launch page with the time you read them. Because the launch link cannot be tagged, attribute visits through referrers in your own analytics and activation after the visit. The [API](https://api.producthunt.com/v2/docs) is token-gated and states it must not be used commercially, so do not automate collection without asking Product Hunt (see [the example import](../examples/observations-product-hunt.csv)).
- **First test.** One launch, one maker present all day, read comments for objections, and compare activations from Product Hunt referrals after seven days against a stated threshold. High rank with no activations is a finding about the product or page.

## YouTube

YouTube suits a game, creative project or learning app whose payoff can be shown in a short demonstration or tutorial, with a tryable link beside it. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. For the AP Government and AP U.S. History study apps, school-age learners do use video, which is also a planning inference, but the audience setting then matters more than the topic: it decides which features and recommendations apply.

- **Rules.** Creators must set whether each video is made for kids, and [YouTube says that is the creator's legal responsibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en) under COPPA and other laws. Content aimed at teens or older is general audience and should be set as not made for kids; content that targets children as one audience among others counts as made for kids. Decide this before upload, and ask counsel if a video is borderline. The [spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en) prohibits misleading titles and thumbnails, repetitive comments that drive traffic, and bought or coerced engagement. Do not use bots or comment automation.
- **Evidence.** [Views](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785?hl=en) count from the moment playback starts and are validated afterwards, so numbers can change. [Impressions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314486?hl=en) count thumbnails shown for over one second with half visible, and impressions click-through rate is how often those led to a view. Record `views` and `impressions` as exposure with the definition and date range; put tagged visits under `clicks` from your own analytics. Watch time has no ledger metric (see [the example import](../examples/observations-youtube.csv)).
- **First test.** Three short videos in one format over two weeks, one tagged link, audience setting chosen first, and a stated stopping rule. Many views with no tagged visits is a finding about the call to action, not a reason to buy views.

## Reddit

Reddit suits a tool, app, game or creative project that has a niche community already discussing the problem, where a useful explanation can start a conversation. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. For the AP Government and AP U.S. History study apps, Reddit's [minimum account age is 13](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043066492-How-old-do-I-need-to-be-to-use-Reddit) (higher in some countries; from search extracts, page not fetched), so students are partly reachable. Treat that as an inference, and never direct a promotion at under-13s.

- **Rules.** Reddit's [spam policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam) prohibits repetitive promotion, and every community adds its own rules, some banning self-promotion entirely. [Reddiquette](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/205926439-Reddiquette) suggests about 1 in 10 of your submissions should be your own content (extract only; page not fetched). Read the community's rules and, where unsure, ask the moderators first. Do not post the same message across communities, use alternate accounts, or buy votes.
- **Evidence.** Reddit's own metric definitions were not verified, so record what you can read on the post. Record `votes` and `comments` read by hand with a definition naming the date and the community, and tagged visits as `clicks` from your own analytics. Votes are a weak demand signal; the objections in the comments are the useful evidence (see [the example import](../examples/observations-reddit.csv)).
- **First test.** One community where you already take part, one honest post with a tagged link, replies handled by hand and logged, and a stated stopping rule. Removal or silence is a result about fit or wording, not a reason to repost.

## Search

Organic web search suits a tool, app or learning product that answers a question people already type, with a page and a working example. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. It has the longest lag of any channel. Google says some changes [take effect in a few hours, others could take several months](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging. For the AP Government and AP U.S. History study apps, students do search for study help, which is an inference; Google's pages fetched here set no age rules for searchers, so any child-directed rules would come from your own site's obligations and were not verified.

- **Rules.** Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) name scaled content abuse (many pages generated mainly to manipulate rankings), doorway abuse, cloaking, keyword stuffing and link spam, and say violating sites may rank lower or not appear. Write a few pages that help a person. Do not mass-produce near-identical pages by city or topic, or buy links.
- **Evidence.** [Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7042828?hl=en) defines an impression as a link shown, a click as a visit to a page outside Google, CTR as clicks divided by impressions, and average position from the topmost result per query. Record `impressions` and `clicks` with that wording. Average position and CTR have no ledger metric; keep them in the definition. [Recent data is preliminary](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17011364?hl=en), and chart and table totals differ (see [the example import](../examples/observations-search.csv)).
- **First test.** One page answering one recurring question, indexed and measured in Search Console for at least six weeks, with a stated stopping rule on impressions, clicks and tagged visits. Impressions with no clicks is a finding about the title and snippet, not a reason to publish more pages.

## Instagram

Instagram suits a project that shows well: a game, creative work, a visual app or a learning product with something to look at in a few seconds. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test, not a promise of reach. It is a Meta property, so Meta's shared rules apply.

- **Rules.** The [Community Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/) cover Instagram and name authenticity as a value; the [spam standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/) prohibits buying, selling or exchanging engagement and posting at very high frequencies manually or automatically. Do not buy followers or run a posting or liking bot. Branded content means content influenced by a business partner for something of value and should use the [branded content tool](https://help.instagram.com/1695974997209192) (read from search extracts; confirm in the app). Promoting your own product is not branded content, but say it is yours. Some content is limited to adults 18 and older under Meta's standards, so check audience settings before aiming at teens. I did not verify rules for links in bios.
- **Evidence.** Instagram's Help Center describes [Insights](https://help.instagram.com/788388387972460) with Accounts reached, Accounts engaged, Content interactions and Plays, with an (i) icon for definitions (search extracts only). Names change, so copy the label your export shows into `definition`. The ledger has no people-reached metric: record Plays as `views`, keep accounts reached in the definition note, and record tagged link visits as `clicks` (see [the example import](../examples/observations-instagram.csv)). Exposure is not first use.
- **First test.** One post with one tagged link and a plain ask, a comments log, and a stopping rule such as seven days or a set number of tagged visits. Silence is a finding about the work or the wording, not a reason to boost it.

## Twitch

The ledger has no average-viewers metric: record follows as `follows`, minutes watched as `hours` (divide by 60, say so), unique viewers as `uniqueVisitors`, and tagged link visits as `clicks` (see [the example import](../examples/observations-twitch.csv)). ## Bluesky

## Apple App Store

The App Store is a destination, not a place to buy attention: people mostly arrive from search, browsing or a link you place elsewhere. It suits an app that is ready to install. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. For the AP study apps, a school-age audience adds constraints: a listing aimed at children has extra rules, and teens sit outside the Kids Category unless you choose it.

- **Rules.** The [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) require metadata that matches the app, bar keyword stuffing, and say that manipulating reviews or inflating rankings with paid, incentivized or fake feedback can end in expulsion. Do not offer rewards for ratings or force a review. Terms such as "For Kids" are reserved for the Kids Category, which discourages third-party analytics and advertising, so decide the audience before choosing analytics. Membership costs [$99 a year](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/program-enrollment); confirm current terms.
- **Evidence.** [App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/acquisition/) reports unique impressions, unique product page views, total downloads (first-time and redownloads) and conversion rate (impressions to downloads), by source type. Record impressions, product page views as `views`, and first-time downloads as `installs` with that definition. [Campaign links](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/campaign-links) tag traffic from your other channels, count a download within 24 hours of the link, and credit only the latest link. The ledger cannot record use, return or revenue from the store, and small counts are hidden below five.
- **First test.** Make one campaign link per outbound channel, send each to the product page for two weeks, and compare product page views and first-time downloads per campaign with a stated stopping rule. Few views is an audience finding; many views and few downloads is a listing finding.

## Google Play

Google Play is a destination store: tagged traffic comes from your other channels, and the store listing converts it. It suits an app that is ready to install. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. For the AP study apps, a school-age audience brings the Families rules into play, so decide the declared audience before choosing ads or analytics.

- **Rules.** [Google Play prohibits manipulating ratings, reviews or install counts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898684?hl=en): no rewards or incentives for ratings, no fake ratings, no forcing a rating, and replies should not ask for a higher one. If children are in the target audience, the [Families requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en) apply: declare the audience before publishing, keep the listing true to the app, use only self-certified ad SDKs and no interest-based ads, and keep the advertising ID off child-only apps. [Registration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en) costs a one-time $25 and new personal accounts have testing and device checks before release; confirm current terms.
- **Evidence.** [Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6263332?hl=en) reports store listing visitors (unique users who visited and have not installed), first-time installers and installs without a listing visit, with UTM-tagged links shown as Tracked channels and untagged links as third-party referrers. Record visitors as `visitors` and first-time installers as `installs` with that definition. Small counts are withheld. The ledger cannot record use, return or revenue from the store.
- **First test.** Tag one outbound link per channel with UTM parameters, send them to the listing for two weeks, and compare visitors and first-time installers by tracked channel with a stated stopping rule. ## Poki

Poki suits a polished browser game with a short loop that works on phones and desktops for a broad casual audience; Poki says its audience is [majority mobile](https://developers.poki.com/guide/what-we-look-for). Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. It is a gate you pass, not a place you post: Poki hand-picks games, so the work is a build that meets its requirements and the tests it runs for you.

- **Rules.** Poki offers [two agreement types](https://developers.poki.com/guide/revenue-deal-types). Web exclusive is its preferred way: on the open web the game is published only on Poki (browser, Discord and YouTube Playables count as web; Steam, mobile app stores and consoles stay yours), by default for 5 years. Non-exclusive is a one-time flat licence fee with no revenue share, for games already live on other web platforms or with a niche or short-term fit. Poki calls the term, the share and the investment indicative and sets the real terms in the agreement, and the page does not say whether an own-site build may stay live under an exclusive deal, so ask before signing. Exclusivity is a choice with a price, not a requirement; [working with Poki](https://developers.poki.com/guide/working-with-poki) adds that direct players earn you 100% of their revenue and Poki-driven players are split 50/50. The [requirements](https://developers.poki.com/guide/requirements-quality) include desktop, mobile and tablet support, 16:9 scaling, an incognito-safe save, correct SDK events, no in-app purchases and no other ad system. The [external resources policy](https://developers.poki.com/guide/external-resources-policy) blocks every external request by default, so analytics need approval and Google Analytics cannot be approved. The [content and AI guidance](https://developers.poki.com/guide/content-player-safety) allows no chat and asks an AI-heavy game to show its own perspective and which tools were used.
- **Evidence.** The [testing steps](https://developers.poki.com/guide/how-testing-works) give Poki's own measures: a player fit test of 500 players that measures playtime only (a healthy result is 3+ minutes average playtime with 25%+ of plays over 3 minutes), then a web fit test of about 7 days that measures click-through rate, time on page and conversion to play against category averages. Record plays as `plays`; Poki reports an average playtime, so record total `hours` only as a derived figure and say so, and keep percentages in `definition` because the ledger stores counts. The pages read do not say what conversion to play divides by, so copy the label you see. After release the dashboard shows earnings per day, and Poki asks you to aim for at least 1 ad per daily active user during the [soft release](https://developers.poki.com/guide/release-process). Poki's own test traffic is not your audience: returns and first use of your own players need analytics Poki has approved (see [the example import](../examples/observations-poki.csv)).
- **First test.** Upload a build for the free playtests and the player fit test (the fit test needs a thumbnail), read playtime against Poki's floor, and move to the web fit test only if it holds. SDK events are required for web fit testing and every release, so budget for them before that step. The [final review](https://developers.poki.com/guide/final-review) takes 1 to 2 weeks and release roughly 2 to 3 months after a pass. A decline is a finding about fit, not a reason to resubmit the same build.

## CrazyGames

CrazyGames suits a polished browser game for players aged 13 and over that reaches gameplay quickly. Treat that fit as a planning inference to test. Its Basic Launch is the cheapest portal test in this library: ads stay off, no revenue is promised, and the platform measures the game with real players for 7 to 21 days.

- **Rules.** The [technical requirements](https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/technical/) allow a total of 250 MB and 1,500 files and an initial download of up to 50 MB (20 MB for the mobile homepage). With the SDK the initial download is measured from the start of loading to the first Gameplay start event; without the SDK the whole upload is used and must be 50 MB or less. A build over 50 MB therefore needs the SDK even for Basic Launch (my inference from those two rules; the [Basic Launch guide](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/) otherwise says no SDK is needed). The [gameplay requirements](https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/gameplay/) ask for English, original content and PEGI 12, for an audience aged 13 and over, and allow no cross-promotion except community links that do not lead to a playable web version, store links for desktop games on the main menu or at the end of a demo, and links within a series; app store links are never allowed in the game. The [FAQ](https://docs.crazygames.com/faq/) says a game already live elsewhere can be published if you hold the rights, that CrazyGames hosts the uploaded files and does not iframe games hosted on competing portals, and that Full Launch earns revenue only through SDK ads. I found no exclusivity rule in the pages read, but the developer terms returned a not-found page when fetched, so read them before relying on that.
- **Evidence.** [Basic Launch ends](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/) once the game has been live for at least 7 days and has 500 plays, or after 21 days, and the dashboard tracks average play time (the average time a player spends in a single session), Day 1 retention (the percentage of players who come back the day after their first session) and conversion (the percentage of players who play at least one minute after starting). Record plays as `plays` and total playtime as derived `hours`. The ledger stores no percentages, so record the cohort's size as `starts` and its day 1 returns as `returns` with the same `cohort` and window, say that the count is derived from the percentage, and read `returnRates` in `report`. The benchmarks CrazyGames quotes (10+ minutes, 10 to 15 percent, 80+ percent) describe successful titles, not a pass mark (see [the example import](../examples/observations-crazygames.csv)).
- **First test.** Preview the build in the Developer Portal, pass QA, run Basic Launch for its 7 to 21 days and compare the three measures with a stated stopping rule before deciding on the SDK and ads for [Full Launch](https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/intro/). Payouts are monthly once earnings reach EUR 100 ([payouts](https://docs.crazygames.com/payouts/)), so Basic Launch earns nothing. Weak retention is a finding about the game, not a reason to add ads.

For Steam showcases and curators, read wishlists and tracked visits on the Steam card. A reply is not coverage and coverage is not first use (see [the example import](../examples/observations-press.csv)).
- **First test.** The press page, then a small hand-picked batch of creators and outlets over two weeks, each message written and sent by you with one tagged link and a stopping rule on replies, not reach. Zero replies from the batch is a finding about the list or the message, not a reason to buy placement.
