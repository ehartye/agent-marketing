# Reception and sentiment

Reception includes what people notice, understand, try, dislike and ask for. Sentiment is one label within that context. The [original IZA employer-review paper](https://docs.iza.org/dp11367.pdf) compares voluntary and incentivized reviews, including a randomized experiment, and demonstrates selection effects. Its employer/MTurk setting does not establish the direction or magnitude of bias in this family's commenters.

Keep exact captured text, source URL, channel, time, theme and review status. The small English keyword heuristic only suggests positive, negative, mixed or unknown. It is not a validated sentiment model; neutral is available for human review. Inspect negation, sarcasm, thread context, translated wording and product-specific meaning. The UI counts only reviewed reactions in its sentiment distribution and shows unreviewed items separately.

Themes should explain an actionable issue: onboarding, device friction, unclear value, pricing, trust, missing capability, delight or return motivation. Distinguish “I dislike AI-made work” from “this feature is broken”; each suggests a different next test. Preserve mixed reactions rather than forcing them into positive or negative. Do not treat a loud thread as a representative market survey.

For a manageable weekly sample, read a few positive, mixed and negative sources, plus feedback from people who did not return. This sampling plan is a house heuristic. Record why the sample was selected and what is missing. A next-step insight names the repeated theme, cites source records, proposes a bounded test and says what outcome would contradict the interpretation.

After recollection, previously reviewed sentiment/themes remain intact. If source text materially changes, recheck the review. Deleted/private sources need special handling: do not assume prior public availability grants permission to republish personal details in collateral.
